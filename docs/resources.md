# Further resources

This page links to the authoritative standards, the regulators and a few freely available third-party resources that complement the kit. The kit itself is original and cites clause, control, criterion and article numbers only; for the real text of a standard, go to the source. Everything below is a link to its owner, not a copy; licences are the owners' own.

## Standards and framework bodies

- **ISO** (iso.org): buy ISO/IEC 27001, ISO/IEC 27002, ISO/IEC 27701 and ISO/IEC 42001 from ISO or your national standards body. The kit maps to these; it does not reproduce them.
- **AICPA** (aicpa.org): the SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria and the description criteria. The criteria text belongs to the AICPA.
- **NIST** (nist.gov): the Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 and the AI Risk Management Framework, both free to download, and SP 800-53 for a deeper control catalogue.
- **European Data Protection Board** (edpb.europa.eu) and the official GDPR text on EUR-Lex: the authoritative regulation and its guidelines.
- **ENISA** (enisa.europa.eu): European guidance on risk and security measures.

## Regulator guidance

- **ICO** (ico.org.uk): practical, plain-language GDPR and data protection guidance, including checklists for small organisations.
- **CNIL** (cnil.fr): guidance, record-of-processing templates and an open-source impact assessment tool.

## Open control and reference material

- **CIS Controls and CIS Benchmarks** (cisecurity.org): a prioritised control set and hardening baselines. Free to read; redistribution of the benchmark files is restricted, so link rather than copy.
- **OWASP** (owasp.org): the Application Security Verification Standard, the Top 10, and the Top 10 for Large Language Model Applications, useful for the secure development and AI risk work.
- **MITRE ATT&CK** (attack.mitre.org) and **MITRE ATLAS** (atlas.mitre.org): adversary technique knowledge bases for threat modelling, ATLAS covering attacks on machine learning systems.

## Freely licensed toolkits worth knowing

These are third-party projects you may use under their own licences. Read each licence before you reuse anything; several are non-commercial or share-alike.

- **ISO27k Toolkit** (iso27001security.com): a community collection of ISMS templates and guidance, mostly under Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike. Good for comparison and ideas; the non-commercial condition limits reuse in paid work.
- **JupiterOne security policy templates** (github.com/JupiterOne/security-policy-templates): a policy and procedure framework under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0.
- **ICT Institute free templates** (ictinstitute.nl): a small set of templates, including an AI policy, under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0.

## A note on HIPAA

The kit does not yet include a HIPAA template set. For United States healthcare, the authoritative source is the HHS Office for Civil Rights (hhs.gov/hipaa), which publishes the Privacy, Security and Breach Notification Rules and small-provider guidance. The kit's access, encryption, logging, incident, risk and vendor documents map closely to the HIPAA Security Rule safeguards, and a dedicated HIPAA mapping is a planned addition.

## How to use these with the kit

Use the standards for the authoritative requirements, the regulators for practical interpretation, and the open control sets for depth. The kit gives you the documents and the structure; these resources give you the detail behind each requirement and the wider community's thinking. None of them replaces reading the standard you intend to certify against.
